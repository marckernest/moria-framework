# **MORIA Framework**

*Product Description*

marckernest.com/moria-framework

# **What It Is**

A personal portfolio landing page that looks and behaves like an immersive Lord of the Rings-style map. The experience is built around the MORIA framework, a systems thinking methodology for problem solving, and it demonstrates that methodology through the design of the experience itself.

The visitor does not read about how Marck thinks. They experience it. The map is the argument.

# **Product Description**

## **The Front Cover**

The visitor lands on what appears to be the front cover of a folded map. It sits closed, slightly worn, as if someone has carried it. The cover contains:

- Title: The MORIA Framework

- Tagline: Journey through MORIA. Where systems thinking meets real solutions.

- Lead line: Lost in the problem? The map is here.

- Cartographer credit: Marck Ernest

- A subtle drag gesture prompt inviting the visitor to open the map

- A secondary prompt to flip the map over and view the back cover

The opening gesture is a horizontal drag or swipe that peels the cover open, revealing the map underneath. On mobile, this is a thumb swipe. The gesture should feel satisfying, with appropriate resistance and a clean snap when released past the threshold. This is the first signal that this is not a standard portfolio site.

## **The Back Cover**

Flipping the closed map reveals the back cover. This side of the experience contains two pieces of content:

- Marck's bio -- the story of how he thinks, what draws him to complex systems, and what he brings to a team that is hard to find elsewhere. Written in a warm, human voice that earns the Gandalf positioning without stating it directly.

- Back cover copy -- a synthesis of the full MORIA journey, closing the experience with a statement about what this framework makes possible and a clear call to action for how to reach Marck.

The back cover should feel like the inside flap of a well-made book. Quiet, considered, and personal. It rewards the visitor who took the time to look.

## **The Open Map**

Once the cover is opened, the visitor finds themselves inside a pannable, zoomable map canvas. The map is rendered in a parchment-style aesthetic, warm and aged, with the feeling that someone has been here before. A dotted journey path connects five city markers across the canvas, each representing a phase of the MORIA framework.

### **The Five Cities**

- The Shire -- Map

- Rivendell -- Orientate

- The Council of Elrond -- Reason

- The Mines of Moria -- Implement

- Lothlorien -- Amplify

### **Map Canvas**

The map canvas is pannable and zoomable on both desktop and mobile. Desktop users click and drag to navigate. Mobile users use their thumbs. The experience is the same on both -- no separate mobile layout. The terrain includes sparse hills, forests, and rivers rendered as SVG in an ink-line cartographic style, flat and minimal, providing context without visual noise.

### **City Markers and Content Panels**

Each city marker is clickable. When clicked, a content panel expands with an ink-bleed animation, revealing the full content for that MORIA phase: a title, subtitle, method steps with Lord of the Rings parallels, and the Joe Melling case study as a grounding example. Closing the panel returns the visitor to the map. Panels can also be navigated sequentially.

### **Visual Language**

- Colour palette: ink (#1a1a18), paper (#f5f2eb), cream (#ede9de), rule (#c8c2b0), accent (#2a5c45), accent-light (#e8f0ec), danger (#8b2e2e)

- Typography: IM Fell English for headings and place names, Crimson Text for body text

- Parchment texture: CSS noise and gradient layering, darker at the edges, lighter at the centre, with visible fold lines

- Map feel: traveled and worn, hand-placed markers, slightly irregular path, imperfect terrain -- signals experience, not newness

# **Process Description**

Every task in this project should be approached with the mindset of a Chief Product Officer. That means:

- Immersion and fluidity of experience are the primary success criteria. A technically correct implementation that breaks the map feeling is a failure.

- Design decisions are held to the question: does this serve the visitor's experience, or does it serve the builder's convenience?

- Each component is considered in the context of the whole. A city marker is not just a button. It is a moment in a journey.

- Performance is part of the experience. A map that lags when panning or stutters when a panel opens is a map that breaks the immersion.

- The first version of anything is a starting point. Every component gets a feel review before it is considered done.

- Content and design inform each other. If a content panel feels wrong inside the map, the solution might be in the copy or in the layout -- both are on the table.
